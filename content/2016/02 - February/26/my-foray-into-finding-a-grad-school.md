So, I've decided to take the plunge into continuing my education.  I've decided that I should probably get a Master's degree in something.  So far I've got it narrowed down to just a few options.  Information Technology, Computer Science, Business Administration.  Interestingly enough I've seen a more than one program that will combine a CS degree with a Juris Doctorate.

For anyone reading this, is there a benefit to getting your JD if you're not going to go into practice?  From what I can gather on-line it can be a bit of a double edged sword.  Some people seem to say that getting a JD but not practicing will just cause you issues during the interview process.  Other people say that if you get a JD and don't practice it still shows that you had the gumption and determination to finish a JD.  Also the process of getting a JD gives you a skill set that you translate into other fields.

This doesn't even get to some of the other things I've been having to think about.  Like what are the best schools in the area?  How am I going to pay for all of this?  Am I qualified to get in?

Finding the best schools hasn't been that hard, what I've been relying on the most is the US News website for college rankings. (<http://grad-schools.usnews.rankingsandreviews.com/best-graduate-schools>)  For paying for school I'll be using my GI Bill and Illinois National Guard Grant.  For qualifications, I think I might be OK.  I graduated with Cum Laude honors, and I have a slew of work and military experience behind me.
